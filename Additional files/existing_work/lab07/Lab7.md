Question: Before AI, explain to a partner what drives your DCF range. Write down what you cannot yet explain about a peer multiple. Expect: a specific question to investigate.
- My DCF valuation is driven primarily by assumptions about future cash flows, growth, the discount rate, and the terminal value. Changes in those assumptions can significantly change the implied value per share.

Question: What is price-to-earnings (P/E)? What do price per share and earnings per share measure? What does a P/E multiple tell you?
- Price per share is the market's current valuation of one share of equity. EPS, or earnings per share, is the company's net income attributable to each diluted share.

QuestionL Why use it? How does comparing earnings help you compare differently sized companies? What does this add to your discounted cash flow valuation?
- The useful thing about dividing price by EPS is that it makes differently sized companies more comparable. AutoNation can be much larger or smaller than Asbury in absolute dollars, but their price relative to earnings can still be compared.

Question: When is it useful—or misleading? What must match between companies? What happens with negative earnings, unusual profits, or different growth prospects?
- In those cases, differences in P/E may reflect real differences in the businesses rather than mispricing.
- A lower P/E does not automatically mean a company is undervalued because differences in growth, risk, profitability, and earnings quality can justify different multiples.

The leave-one-out result confirmed this: with only AutoNation remaining, the implied price decreased to $215.81, a change of -$15.18 from the full-peer estimate.

Evolve: Removing Group 1 lowers the implied value because it removes the higher-multiple peer from the comparison. With only AutoNation remaining, there is only one peer multiple, so $215.81 is a reference estimate rather than a range. A range requires multiple peer observations that create a spread between lower and higher multiples.

Reflect: P/E measures how much investors are paying for each dollar of a company's earnings by dividing price per share by diluted earnings per share. It is useful for comparing companies of different sizes because it puts price relative to earnings on a common basis.
