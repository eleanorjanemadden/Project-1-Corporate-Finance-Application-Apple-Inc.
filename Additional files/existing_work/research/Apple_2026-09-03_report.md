## Investment thesis

My current view is **watch-defer**: do not initiate a position at the valuation date, but keep Apple under active coverage while testing the conditions that could justify entry. **[Author judgment]**

Apple combines what I assess to be a large installed base with a growing Services business, strong operating profitability, and substantial cash generation. **[Installed-base characterization: Author assessment requiring an additional source; Services growth: S1, PDF p. 31 and S4, PDF p. 1; operating profitability and cash generation: S1, PDF pp. 40 and 43 and S4, PDF pp. 1 and 3]** FY2026 results through the third quarter strengthen the operating case, but the reference trailing P/E of approximately 37.3x still embeds demanding expectations and leaves limited room for slower hardware growth, margin pressure, regulatory changes to Services economics, or weak returns from artificial-intelligence investment. **[FY2026 operating evidence: S4, PDF pp. 1 and 3; valuation input: S3; risk assessment: Author judgment informed by S1, PDF pp. 17-21]** The available evidence supports continued research, but it does not yet establish an attractive forward return with a defensible margin of safety. **[Author judgment]**

I would consider moving the thesis to **initiate** if further research supports durable high-single-digit or better earnings growth, resilient Services economics, and an acceptable expected return at the purchase price. **[Author judgment]** I would move it toward **do not initiate** if installed-base monetization weakens, hardware replacement cycles lengthen structurally, margins deteriorate, or the valuation case depends mainly on the market maintaining or expanding the current multiple. **[Author judgment]**

## Evidence

- FY2025 operating income was **$133.050 billion**, net income was **$112.010 billion**, and diluted EPS was **$7.46**. **[S1, PDF p. 40]**
- Weighted-average diluted shares fell to **15.005 billion** from 15.408 billion in FY2024. **[S1, PDF p. 48]** I view that decline as evidence that repurchases continued to support per-share results. **[Author judgment based on S1, PDF pp. 48 and 59]**
- At September 27, 2025, Apple held **$35.934 billion** of cash and cash equivalents and **$96.486 billion** of current and non-current marketable securities. **[S1, PDF pp. 42 and 49; marketable securities equal $18.763 billion current plus $77.723 billion non-current]**

- Services revenue increased to **$109.158 billion** in FY2025 from $96.169 billion in FY2024, while Products revenue increased to **$307.003 billion** from $294.866 billion. **[S1, PDF p. 40]**
- Apple repurchased **402 million shares for $89.3 billion** during FY2025. **[S1, PDF p. 59]**

## Consequential assumptions to test

The recommendation depends on the following assumptions; these are analytical premises rather than established facts. **[Author assumptions]**

- Services can continue growing faster than Products without a material reduction in its economics from regulation, platform-policy changes, or mix. **[Author assumption informed by S1, PDF pp. 17-21 and 31]**
- Apple's installed base remains loyal and monetizable even if device replacement cycles lengthen. **[Author assumption informed by S1, PDF pp. 1 and 6]**
- Gross and operating margins remain broadly resilient despite tariffs, component costs, competitive investment, and product mix. **[Author assumption informed by S1, PDF pp. 17-21 and 32-33]**
- Repurchases create per-share value rather than merely offset dilution or destroy value at an excessive purchase price. **[Author assumption informed by S1, PDF pp. 48 and 59]**
- Apple's artificial-intelligence spending and product strategy eventually produce incremental revenue, stronger retention, or operating efficiencies. **[Author assumption]**
- Apple retains a premium valuation if earnings growth and returns on capital remain superior; the case does not require further multiple expansion. **[Author assumption using S3 as the starting valuation]**
- Cash and marketable securities beyond operating needs are economically available to capital providers, subject to tax, liquidity, and management-allocation constraints. **[Author assumption informed by S1, PDF pp. 42 and 49]**

## Material unknowns

The current evidence does not resolve the following questions. **[Author assessment of evidence gaps]**

- The sustainable organic growth rate and incremental margin of Services by major revenue source.
- The size, engagement, replacement behavior, and geographic economics of the installed base.
- The earnings and cash-flow effect of AI-related capital expenditure and operating expense.
- The effect of App Store, search-distribution, and other regulatory or legal outcomes on Services revenue and margins.
- The durability of iPhone demand and pricing, especially in Greater China.
- The normalized tariff, supply-chain, foreign-exchange, and component-cost burden.
- FY2026 free cash flow, net cash, diluted share count, and forward earnings power as of the valuation date.
- Management's hurdle rate and likely pace of repurchases at the current share price.
- The earnings level and valuation multiple appropriate for a credible bear case.

## Research plan

1. Update the financial model through the latest available FY2026 quarterly filing, separating Products and Services growth and margins where the evidence permits.
2. Build base, bull, and bear cases for revenue, operating margin, diluted shares, free cash flow, and EPS over five years.
3. Reconcile market capitalization to enterprise value using current diluted shares, debt, cash, marketable securities, and other non-operating claims or assets.
4. Value Apple with a discounted cash-flow model and cross-check it against forward P/E, EV/EBIT, and free-cash-flow yield.
5. Test Services exposure to App Store remedies, search-distribution changes, geographic mix, and lower-margin service categories.
6. Assess installed-base health using company disclosures, third-party device and usage data, replacement-cycle evidence, and customer-retention indicators.
7. Evaluate China demand, supply-chain concentration, tariffs, foreign exchange, and competitive product risk.
8. Review capital allocation by comparing historical repurchase prices and share-count reduction with estimates of intrinsic value.
9. Define explicit initiation conditions, including the required expected return, downside tolerance, and margin of safety, and identify evidence that would close or permanently reject the idea.
