# Project 1 - Edition A: Apple Inc. (AAPL)

## Decision and user

This analysis supports the fund's investment committee in deciding whether to initiate a position in Apple Inc. The initial recommendation is **watch-defer**: do not initiate at the valuation date, but continue research and establish conditions that could justify entry.

## Company and valuation date

- **Company:** Apple Inc. (Nasdaq: AAPL)
- **Valuation date:** September 1, 2026
- **Reference market price:** $325.13 per share
- **Reference market capitalization:** approximately $4.784 trillion
- **Reference trailing P/E:** approximately 37.3x

Market data are a point-in-time reference from the finance quote retrieved on September 1, 2026. The fundamental evidence below comes from Apple's FY2025 Form 10-K, filed October 31, 2025.

## Initial thesis: watch-defer

Apple is an exceptional company with a large installed base, a growing Services business, strong operating profitability, and substantial cash generation. However, the reference valuation embeds demanding expectations: a roughly 37.3x trailing P/E leaves limited room for slower hardware growth, margin pressure, regulatory changes to Services economics, or weaker returns from artificial-intelligence investment. The current evidence is sufficient to keep Apple under active coverage, but not sufficient to establish an attractive forward return with a defensible margin of safety.

The thesis could move to **initiate** if research shows durable high-single-digit or better earnings growth, resilient Services economics, and a credible path to acceptable returns at the purchase price. It could move to **do not initiate** if installed-base monetization weakens, hardware replacement cycles lengthen structurally, margins deteriorate, or valuation support depends mainly on multiple persistence or expansion.

## Known evidence and sources

- Apple reported FY2025 net sales of **$416.161 billion**, up 6% from $391.035 billion in FY2024. Products contributed $307.003 billion and Services contributed $109.158 billion.
- FY2025 operating income was **$133.050 billion**, net income was **$112.010 billion**, and diluted EPS was **$7.46**.
- Weighted-average diluted shares were **15.005 billion**, down from 15.408 billion in FY2024, evidencing continued per-share support from repurchases.
- At September 27, 2025, Apple held **$35.934 billion** of cash and cash equivalents and **$96.486 billion** of marketable securities.
- Carrying debt was **$98.657 billion**, comprising $7.979 billion of commercial paper and $90.678 billion of term debt.
- Services generated **$109.158 billion** of FY2025 revenue, up from $96.169 billion in FY2024, while Products revenue rose to $307.003 billion from $294.866 billion.
- Apple repurchased **402 million shares for $89.3 billion** during FY2025.
- The reference price of $325.13, market capitalization of approximately $4.784 trillion, and trailing P/E of approximately 37.3x were retrieved for the September 1, 2026 valuation date.

## Consequential assumptions

- Services can continue growing faster than Products without a material reduction in its economics from regulation, platform-policy changes, or mix.
- Apple's installed base remains loyal and monetizable even if device replacement cycles lengthen.
- Gross and operating margins remain broadly resilient despite tariffs, component costs, competitive investment, and product mix.
- Repurchases continue to create per-share value rather than merely offset dilution or destroy value at an excessive valuation.
- Apple's artificial-intelligence spending and product strategy produce either incremental revenue, stronger retention, or operating efficiencies.
- The market will continue assigning Apple a premium multiple if earnings growth and return on capital remain superior; the investment case does not require further multiple expansion.
- Cash and marketable securities in excess of operating needs are economically available to capital providers, subject to tax, liquidity, and management-allocation constraints.

## Unknowns

- The sustainable organic growth rate and incremental margin of Services by major revenue source.
- The size, engagement, replacement behavior, and geographic economics of the current installed base.
- The earnings and cash-flow effect of AI-related capital expenditure and operating expense.
- The impact of App Store, search-distribution, and other regulatory or legal outcomes on Services revenue and margins.
- The durability of iPhone demand and pricing, especially in Greater China.
- The normalized tariff, supply-chain, foreign-exchange, and component-cost burden.
- FY2026 free cash flow, net cash, diluted share count, and forward earnings power at the valuation date.
- Management's hurdle rate and likely pace for repurchases at the current share price.
- The downside valuation multiple and earnings level under a credible bear case.

## Research plan

1. Update the financial model through the latest available FY2026 quarterly filing, separating Products and Services growth and margins where evidence permits.
2. Build base, bull, and bear cases for revenue, operating margin, diluted shares, free cash flow, and EPS over five years.
3. Reconcile market capitalization to enterprise value using current diluted shares, debt, cash, marketable securities, and other non-operating claims or assets.
4. Value Apple with a discounted cash-flow model and cross-check it against forward P/E, EV/EBIT, and free-cash-flow yield.
5. Test Services exposure to App Store remedies, search-distribution changes, geographic mix, and lower-margin service categories.
6. Assess installed-base health using company disclosures, third-party device and usage data, replacement-cycle evidence, and customer retention indicators.
7. Evaluate China demand, supply-chain concentration, tariffs, foreign exchange, and competitive product risk.
8. Review capital allocation by measuring historical repurchase prices and share-count reduction against intrinsic-value estimates.
9. Define explicit initiation conditions, including a required expected return, downside tolerance, and margin of safety; identify evidence that would close or permanently reject the idea.
